Why We're Doing This

We are not lawyers. We are people who have worked in the housing industry — contractors, maintenance techs, property workers, past employees, and even some active employees — and we have seen enough.

Where We Come From

Years of working inside occupied rentals. This includes past employees and active employees who have seen the problems firsthand. Walking into homes with mold hidden behind paint, water damage covered up instead of fixed, and repairs done by unlicensed companies that left conditions worse. We watched complaints get ignored, tenants get sick, and people who spoke up get cut off from work.

What We've Seen

Corporate landlords and large property managers treating homes as pure financial assets. Delayed repairs. Cover-ups. Fraudulent or incomplete work. Families living in conditions that no one should have to accept while the companies continue to collect rent.

Our Why

Americans deserve better. Safe, habitable homes are not optional. We started this project to document the patterns, give renters practical tools, and make it harder for slumlords to profit from neglect. We are not here to give legal advice — we are here because we’ve seen the fraud up close and decided enough is enough.

About the Project

IMPORTANT: We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. We are a group of people who have worked in the housing industry (contractors, maintenance, property work) and have seen enough of the fraud, cover-ups, unlicensed work, mold, and neglect going on. We document patterns and help renters organize evidence.

The American Housing Accountability Project exists to protect renters and demand accountability from landlords — especially large corporate owners, hedge funds, and single-family rental giants that prioritize profits over safe, habitable homes.

Too many families live with mold, water damage, delayed repairs, unlicensed work, and ignored habitability issues. We document patterns, support renters with practical tools, and push for real consequences when housing standards are ignored.

We Stand For

  • Safe, mold-free, livable homes

  • Transparent ownership and accountability
  • Responsible small landlords and property managers
  • Renters who refuse to accept substandard conditions

Delayed or Denied Repairs

Repeated requests ignored while conditions deteriorate. Habitability is not optional.

Renter Resources

This is general information only — not legal advice. We are not lawyers. Laws vary by state. Consult a local attorney or housing authority for your specific situation.

Document Everything

  • Take dated photos and videos of all problems (wide shots + close-ups)
  • Keep a written log of when issues started, who you notified, and responses
  • Save all texts, emails, and certified mail receipts
  • Note any health symptoms and medical visits related to conditions

Give Proper Written Notice

Send clear written notice describing the problem, its location, and what needs to be fixed. Certified mail creates a strong paper trail. Give reasonable time for response before escalating.

Know Habitability Basics

Most states have an implied warranty of habitability requiring landlords to keep units safe and livable (working plumbing, heat, no major structural/moisture issues, etc.). Significant mold from landlord-caused moisture is widely treated as a habitability problem.

Report to Authorities

Contact local code enforcement, health department, or housing authority. Document their inspections. For serious issues, consider legal aid or tenant rights organizations in your state.
